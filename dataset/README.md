# GoHumanize Open Humanizer Dataset

2,957 training pairs and 300 test pairs for teaching a language model to rewrite
AI-styled English prose into natural human writing. Each pair is:

- `input`: a passage rewritten by a large language model in the register typical of LLM output
  (formal, smooth, hedged, connective phrases, no contractions);
- `output`: the original human-written passage, from a public-domain book or, since version 2,
  from a US federal government publication.

The human side is the target. A model trained on this data learns the reverse direction:
from AI-styled prose back to the human original.

Built for the [GoHumanize Open Humanizer](https://gohumanize.ai/open-model), an educational
open model. It is separate from the production systems of GoHumanize.ai and makes no claim
about AI detectors.

## Sources and licence

All human text is in the public domain in the United States.

- **Books (2,000 train, 200 test)**: **Project Gutenberg** books that are English-language
  originals published before 1929. Translations were excluded on purpose (a translation can
  carry a newer copyright than the original work). Project Gutenberg boilerplate, footnote
  markers, illustration tags and typographic artefacts were stripped.
- **Modern prose (957 train, 100 test), added in version 2**: news releases, features and speeches
  from four US federal agencies. Works of the US federal government are not subject to copyright
  in the United States (17 U.S.C. 105). Site banners, navigation, press-release datelines,
  footnote numbers and media notices were stripped or dropped.

| Agency | Train rows |
|---|---|
| National Institute of Standards and Technology | 398 |
| Federal Reserve | 350 |
| NASA | 198 |
| Department of Energy | 11 |

Passages are groups of consecutive paragraphs of 80 to 300 words.

The AI-styled inputs were generated by three models with four rotating style prompts. For the
modern passages a stricter instruction was used, which keeps every fact, name and number but
forbids keeping the sentence structure: with the original instruction, AI-styled versions of
modern factual text came back so close to the original that the pairs taught the model to leave
modern text unchanged (see the write-up, section 6).

|---|---|---|
| gpt-4o-mini | 1516 | 201 |
| meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct | 721 | 58 |
| deepseek-chat | 720 | 41 |

## Statistics

| | Train | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Rows | 2957 | 300 |
| Book passages (fiction / non-fiction) | 2000 (1468 / 532) | 200 (142 / 58) |
| Modern passages | 957 | 100 |
| Books / articles | 47 / 532 | 47 / 59 |
| Avg. input words | 205.1 | 207.1 |
| Avg. output words | 177.7 | 179.1 |

At most 46 passages per book in training, so no single author dominates. The book test passages
come from the same books; they measure the transformation, not generalisation to unseen authors.
The modern test passages come from 59 articles that never appear in training, so no test article
has sibling passages the model has seen.

## Fields

`id`, `input`, `output`, `generator`, `style`, `source` (`gutenberg` or `us-federal`),
`gutenberg_id`, `title`, `author`, `category` (`fiction`, `nonfiction` or `modern`), `url`,
`input_words`, `output_words`. For modern rows `gutenberg_id` is 0, `title` and `url` are the
article's address and `author` is the agency.

## How it was built

The full pipeline (sourcing, cleaning, selection, AI-fication, this card) is in
https://github.com/GoHumanize-ai/gohumanize-open-humanizer under `pipeline/`. The write-up
explaining every step is in `docs/paper.md` there.

## Known limitations

- Two thirds of the human prose is old (pre-1929) and the rest is government writing, so the
  "human" style skews literary on one side and official on the other. Casual modern writing is
  not represented.
- The agency sites change daily; re-running the collection gets different articles. This
  dataset is the fixed record of the articles used.
- English only. Passages are 80 to 300 words; longer documents are out of scope.
- The AI side reflects three specific generators and four prompts, not all AI writing.
